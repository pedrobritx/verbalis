## 1. British English mechanics (EN target)

Default to **British English** for all EN output unless the client specifies otherwise.

- **Spelling**: -ise/-isation (organise, organisation, realise), -our (labour, behaviour,
  honour), -re (centre, theatre, metre), -ogue (catalogue, dialogue), -ce nouns vs -se verbs
  (licence/license, practice/practise, defence), travelled/-lling (double l), programme
  (not program, except computer programs), analyse, cheque, enrol, fulfil, instalment, judgement
  (general) vs judgment (often used in legal/court contexts — keep consistent and note choice).
- **Quotation marks**: single quotes ‘…’ as primary, "…" nested inside. Punctuation goes
  outside the closing quote unless it belongs to the quoted material (logical punctuation).
- **Dates**: 18 June 2026 / 18/06/2026 (day-month-year). Never the US m/d/y order.
- **Collective nouns**: may take plural verbs (the Government are / the committee have) —
  acceptable in BrE but keep agreement consistent within a document.
- **Legal register**: prefer "claimant" (not "plaintiff", post-1999 CPR), "defendant",
  "counsel", "barrister/solicitor". For competition law use CMA/UK terminology where the
  text is UK-facing (see §8 on jurisdiction).
- Avoid Americanisms: "whilst/while" both fine; prefer "shall" only where legal force intended.

## 2. Brazilian Portuguese mechanics (PT target)

- **Orthography**: current *Acordo Ortográfico* (post-2009) — no trema except in proper
  names/derivatives; reduced use of hyphens and accents (e.g. *ideia*, *voo*, *leem*).
- **Decimal/thousands**: comma decimal, point (or thin space) thousands — 1.234.567,89.
- **Dates**: 18 de junho de 2026 — month lowercase. Numeric: 18/06/2026.
- **Legal register**: maintain formal *tratamento* (Vossa Excelência for the judiciary,
  Vossa Senhoria for officials) and the heavy nominalised style of Brazilian legalese only
  to the degree the source carries it; do not invent formality the source lacks.
- **Capitalisation**: institution names and *órgãos* capitalised (Superintendência-Geral,
  Tribunal, União); generic references lowercased.

## 3. Numbers, dates, currency, units (both directions)

| Item | Brazilian PT | British EN |
|------|--------------|------------|
| Decimal separator | comma (3,14) | point (3.14) |
| Thousands separator | point / space (1.000) | comma (1,000) |
| Date | 18/06/2026; 18 de junho de 2026 | 18/06/2026; 18 June 2026 |
| Currency | R$ 1.234,56 | £1,234.56 |
| Billion | 10⁹ (*bilhão* = 10⁹) | 10⁹ (modern short scale) — verify, historic long-scale traps |
| Percent | 50% (space optional: 50 %) | 50% (no space) |

- **Currency**: do **not** convert amounts; keep source currency and symbol, transcribe
  faithfully. If clarification is genuinely needed, add a translator's note, never a silent
  conversion.
- **"Bilhão/trilhão"**: Brazil uses short scale (bilhão = 10⁹), matching modern English
  "billion". Older texts or European-PT sources may differ — verify against figures in context.
- **Units**: Brazil is metric; keep metric. Only convert if the target text's purpose requires
  it and then add both (e.g. "5 km (≈3.1 miles)") with a note.

## 4. ABNT / NBR documentary & citation standards

Brazilian academic and technical documents follow **ABNT** (Associação Brasileira de Normas
Técnicas) norms. When translating academic/technical texts **to** Portuguese, or when a PT
source's references must be preserved, respect these. The key norms:

- **NBR 6023 — References** (bibliographic reference lists). A **new 3rd edition, NBR 6023:2025,
  was released in May 2025**, superseding NBR 6023:2018 (which itself replaced 2002). Many
  institutions still mandate the 2018 edition — confirm which edition applies before reformatting.
  Distinctive ABNT features: author SURNAME in capitals; **title in bold** (not italics);
  subtitle after colon not bold; location before publisher; author-date *or* numeric in-text systems.
- **NBR 10520 — In-text citations / footnotes**. Current edition **NBR 10520:2023**. Governs
  (AUTOR, ano) author-date calls and footnote citations.
- **NBR 6022 — Articles** in technical/scientific periodicals (structure of a paper).
- **NBR 6028 — Abstracts** (*resumo*) presentation.
- **NBR 6024 — Section numbering**; **NBR 6027 — table of contents (*sumário*)**;
  **NBR 14724 — academic work presentation (*trabalhos acadêmicos*: TCC, dissertação, tese)**.

**Translation rule of thumb**: ABNT vs international styles (APA/MLA/Chicago) are *not*
interchangeable. When converting a reference list across languages, ask whether the target
document should retain ABNT (PT-facing) or adopt the target-culture style (EN-facing). Default:
preserve the source's citation system and flag if conversion is wanted. Never silently restyle
citations.

## 5. ISO conventions relevant to translation

- **ISO 17100** — requirements for translation services (the revision/QA workflow this skill
  follows mirrors its translate → revise → review stages; it underpins the four-eyes principle).
- **ISO 18587** — post-editing of machine translation output (full vs light PE) — relevant when
  the source is a draft/MT output to be revised.
- **ISO 639** — language codes (pt-BR, en-GB). Use these in metadata/filenames.
- **ISO 3166** — country codes (BR, GB). **ISO 4217** — currency codes (BRL, GBP, USD, EUR);
  prefer the symbol in running text, the code in tables/data.
- **ISO 8601** — date/time (2026-06-18). Use in data, logs, structured fields; use the local
  long form in prose.
- **ISO 2384** — presentation of translations (bibliographic identification of the translated
  document) — relevant for title pages of formal translated documents.

## 6. Sworn / certified translation (*tradução juramentada*) formatting

The Brazilian sworn translator — officially **tradutor público e intérprete comercial** — is
governed by Lei nº 14.195/2021 (Ch. VII, arts. 18–27), historically Decreto nº 13.609/1943,
and registered with the state **Junta Comercial**. Sworn translations carry *fé pública* and
must mirror the source. Even outside a formal sworn context, these conventions are the gold
standard for legal/official documents:

- **Faithful, complete rendering** — nothing omitted, nothing added. Every seal, stamp,
  signature, letterhead, marginal note, and illegible passage is **described in brackets**,
  not silently dropped: `[brasão da República]`, `[assinatura ilegível]`, `[carimbo: ...]`,
  `[stamp: ...]`, `[illegible]`.
- **Do not translate**: proper names, registered company names, and addresses stay as-is
  (may add a bracketed gloss on first occurrence if meaning matters).
- **Document apparatus**: open with a heading identifying it as a translation from X into Y;
  number pages; close with the translator's certification clause. Reproduce the **layout**
  (headings, tables, ordering) as closely as the target language allows.
- **Numbers, registration codes, dates**: transcribe exactly. CPF/CNPJ, processo numbers,
  *matrícula*, etc. are copied verbatim, never reformatted.
- **Untranslatables / institution names**: keep the original and add a functional gloss or
  translator's note rather than forcing a misleading equivalent (see §8).

## 7. Document-type quick rules

- **Judicial filings, powers of attorney, deeds, wills, contracts** → formal equivalence;
  documentary fidelity; preserve legal effect; sworn-style bracketed annotation of apparatus.
- **Statutes / regulations** → legislative terminology first; preserve article/§/inciso
  structure (*artigo, parágrafo, inciso, alínea* ↔ article, paragraph, item, sub-item — verify).
- **Competition-law / CADE documents** → CADE termbase is authoritative for PT↔EN; map
  institutional names per §8; keep AC/CADE/SG acronyms with a gloss on first use.
- **Economic / financial reports** → dynamic equivalence for readability; regulatory term
  usage; faithful figures; keep currency.
- **Academic / technical** → respect ABNT (§4) for PT; ISO terminology; consistent term use.
- **Journalistic / public-facing** → covert translation, natural readability, accuracy over
  literalism, but no spin added.
- **Certificates, transcripts, passports, civil registry** → sworn-style; bracket all apparatus;
  transcribe IDs verbatim; standard equivalents for *certidão*, *averbação*, *histórico escolar*.

## 8. Institutional & jurisdictional equivalence cautions

Legal/institutional concepts rarely map one-to-one across Brazil, the UK, the EU, and the US.
Resolve **concepts, not words**, and prefer the terminology of the *target reader's* jurisdiction
when the text is jurisdiction-facing; otherwise keep the source institution name + functional gloss.

- **Competition authority**: CADE (Conselho Administrativo de Defesa Econômica) ↔ describe as
  "Brazil's competition authority"; the UK analogue is the CMA, the EU the European Commission
  (DG COMP), the US the FTC/DOJ Antitrust Division — these are *analogues, not translations*.
  Keep "CADE" and gloss; do not call CADE "the CMA".
- **Courts**: STF, STJ, TST, TRF, *vara*, *tribunal de alçada* have no exact UK/US equals —
  keep the Portuguese name and add a descriptive gloss on first use.
- **Sham litigation, leniency, merger control, abuse of dominant position** — these *do* have
  settled CADE↔EN equivalents (in the glossary); use them.
- When several valid renderings exist, follow the skill's terminological-ambiguity protocol:
  list variants, explain the jurisdictional difference, recommend one, rank the rest. Never
  present competing institutional terms as interchangeable without evidence.

**Authority hierarchy for verification** (highest first):
- Brazil: CADE, federal government publications, regulatory agencies, courts.
- UK: GOV.UK, legislation.gov.uk, CMA, FCA, NAO.
- EU: EUR-Lex, European Commission. US: FTC, DOJ. International: OECD, IMF, World Bank, WTO.
